We study the limitations of the proximity-gaps property for general linear codes. It has long
been known [BGKS20] that every linear codeC of relative distanceδ satisfies the proximity-gaps
property at any radius below
J3/2(δ) := 1−(1−δ) 1/3.
This distance is often called the one-and-a-half Johnson radius and despite much attention, it still
stands as the best known radius for the proximity-gaps property of general linear codes (and, as
we show in this paper, there is a good reason for this). On the other hand, certain families of
codes are known to satisfy much stronger proximity-gaps properties. Reed–Solomon codes have the
proximity-gaps property up to the Johnson radius1− (1−δ )1/2, rather than only the one-and-a-half
Johnson radius [BCI+23,BCH +25], while random linear codes, folded Reed-Solomon codes, and
random Reed-Solomon codes have the proximity-gaps property all the way up to capacity, i.e., radius
1−R for a code of rateR (asymptotically equal toδ for Reed–Solomon codes) [GG25,GGSW26].
The stronger proximity-gaps property of Reed–Solomon codes, along with their optimal rate–distance
tradeoff, are the main reason that Reed–Solomon codes are the predominant choice in hash-based
SNARKs [BBHR18,ACFY24,ACFY25]. At a high level, these properties allow Reed–Solomon-based
SNARKs to achieve the smallest proof sizes among hash-based SNARKs.
On the other hand, several works have proposed hash-based proof systems based on other
linear codes in order to obtain desirable properties such as faster encoding or the ability to work
over different fields [GLS+23,XZS22,BCF +25,ZCF24,AGK +26]. According to existing analyses,
however, the proximity tests in these proof systems are limited to the one-and-a-half Johnson radius
leading to larger proof sizes compared to proof systems using Reed-Solomon codes. Many authors
have thus considered the possibility that broader classes of linear codes might satisfy stronger,
Reed–Solomon-like proximity gaps.1 In particular, Diamond and Gruen [DG24] conjectured that
every linear code of relative distanceδ satisfies proximity gaps up to its unique-decoding radiusδ/2.

1.2 Technical overview

steps, starting with a small-distance base construction and then amplifying distance using standard

1.3 Comparison with prior work on counterexamples
The work [BGKS20] gives a proximity-gaps counterexample at(δ,ρ ) = (7 /8, 1/2). That is, a
code with distance7/8that does not satisfy the proximity-gaps property for any radius above
J3/2(7/8) = 1 /2. This gives one distance δ where the one-and-a-half Johnson radius is tight,
but [BGKS20] does not give any counterexamples at other distances. Diamond and Gruen [DG24]
constructed a Reed–Solomon example at the unique-decoding radius with exactlyn exceptional
coefficients. The presumed tightness of this example (not only among Reed-Solomon codes but also
amongst all linear codes) is what motivated their conjecture that all linear codes satisfy proximity
gaps up to their unique-decoding radius, with at mostnexceptional coefficients.
5
ForthespecialclassofReed–Solomoncodes, anumberofrecentworkshavegivencounterexamples
at various distances, but the distances of these counterexamples are near capacity [DG25,Kam26,
KKH26]. Their intention is to show the limits of the recent Grand MCA Challenge [ABF26]
which conjectures that Reed–Solomon codes satisfy proximity gaps all the way up to capacity (or
equivalently, up to their distanceδ).
Our work is orthogonal to this line of research on Reed–Solomon counterexamples as we focus
on general linear codes.
